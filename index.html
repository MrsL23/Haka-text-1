What is a haka?

A haka is a Māori composition performed with the body — voice, face, hands, feet — by a group moving as one. It is not only a war dance, and it is not only performed before rugby matches.

Haka are performed to welcome visitors, to challenge, to , to celebrate, to mark an occasion, and to say something a speech cannot. The best-known haka in the world, Ka Mate, is not a war dance at all: it is a composition about survival.

Can women perform a haka?

Yes. Women perform haka, lead haka, and compose them. The question is asked often because the haka most people have seen is performed by a men’s rugby team, which is not a representative example of the art.

There are distinctions within the form — whetero is performed by men, and particular haka belong to particular groups and occasions with their own protocols. But the idea that haka is a men’s art is a misinterpretation produced by television.
